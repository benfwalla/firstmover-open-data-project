## Summary

July 2026 recorded **25,442 new rental listings** in New York City, only 61 fewer than June. Citywide median asking rent reached **$4,000** for the first time in 2026, but the $5 monthly increase was effectively flat. Renters saw another month of high summer inventory without broad citywide price relief.

Across 158 neighborhoods with comparable data, the median month-over-month rent change was **+0.5%**. Larger apartments got more expensive even as the overall market held steady. Both 2BR and 3BR medians rose $145.

The full dataset, 25,442 listings with 34 columns each, is available on the [Open Data](/open-data) page.

## By Bedroom Count

| Type | Listings | Median Rent | vs. June |
|------|----------|-------------|----------|
| Studio | 4,116 | $3,383 | -$12 |
| 1 Bedroom | 8,900 | $3,750 | +$50 |
| 2 Bedroom | 8,094 | $4,495 | +$145 |
| 3 Bedroom | 3,410 | $4,795 | +$145 |

Studios were the only major category to get cheaper, and only by $12. One-bedrooms rose $50. The sharper moves came in family-sized inventory, where both 2BR and 3BR medians climbed $145 even as their listing counts increased.

## Biggest Price Movements

Among neighborhoods with at least 10 listings in both June and July:

**Largest drops:** Noho (-21%), Hudson Square (-19%), Central Park South (-15%), Stuyvesant Town/PCV (-19%), Carnegie Hill (-13%)

**Largest increases:** Vinegar Hill (+20%), Kingsbridge Heights (+54%), Fort Lee (+40%), Sutton Place (+17%), Manhattanville (+13%)

Several of July's biggest movements reversed June's direction. Noho, Hudson Square, and Stuyvesant Town/PCV all gave back sharp June gains, while Manhattanville and Sutton Place rebounded from June declines. Bushwick led volume again with 1,340 listings, but Williamsburg narrowed the gap to 111. Among other busy neighborhoods, Astoria rose 6% to a $3,499 median and the Upper West Side rose 5% to $4,725.
