## Summary

June 2026 recorded **25,503 new rental listings** in New York City, up 13% from May's 22,662 and the busiest month of 2026 so far. The extra inventory barely moved the citywide median asking rent. It reached **$3,995**, just $10 above May and close enough to call the market flat.

Across 152 neighborhoods with comparable data, the median month-over-month rent change was **0%**. The largest swings were concentrated in smaller submarkets: Middle Village fell 22%, Secaucus dropped 21%, and Red Hook rose 41%.

The full dataset, 25,503 listings with 34 columns each, is available on the [Open Data](/open-data) page.

## By Bedroom Count

| Type | Listings | Median Rent | vs. May |
|------|----------|-------------|---------|
| Studio | 4,178 | $3,395 | +$95 |
| 1 Bedroom | 9,041 | $3,700 | -$50 |
| 2 Bedroom | 7,955 | $4,350 | +$50 |
| 3 Bedroom | 3,370 | $4,650 | -$50 |

More apartments came to market in every major bedroom category, but prices moved in different directions. Studios posted the biggest increase at $95. One-bedrooms and 3BRs each slipped $50, while 2BRs gained $50. That mix explains why the citywide median barely changed despite the surge in supply.

## Biggest Price Movements

Among neighborhoods with at least 10 listings in both May and June:

**Largest drops:** Manhattanville (-19%), Secaucus (-21%), Middle Village (-22%), Sutton Place (-12%), Bensonhurst (-20%)

**Largest increases:** Noho (+34%), Red Hook (+41%), Stuyvesant Town/PCV (+21%), Hudson Square (+14%), Clinton Hill (+13%)

Bushwick remained the highest-volume neighborhood with 1,330 new listings, 181 more than Williamsburg. East Village held third place with 897. Most high-volume neighborhoods stayed relatively stable on price, even as the city added thousands of listings. The biggest moves came from smaller markets where a few expensive or inexpensive apartments can shift the median quickly.
